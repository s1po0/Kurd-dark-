## 1.1 — Title, Tagline & Positioning

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| **Full Title** | **DARK SOULS: CONVERGENCE OF ASH & WANT** |
| **Short form / marketing** | *Dark Souls: Convergence* |
| **Tagline (EN)** | *"Three ages remembered the world. Only one of them was right."* |
| **Tagline (alt, key art)** | *"You are what the fire left behind."* |
| **In-fiction title** | *The Convergence* — used by NPCs; the player never hears the word "game" |
| **Title-card treatment** | No subtitle on boot. The words **DARK SOULS** ignite in ember-orange, then a grey ash-front sweeps left-to-right and leaves **CONVERGENCE OF ASH & WANT** etched in cold silver beneath. Audio: one bell toll, one exhalation, one match-strike. |

**Positioning statement.** *Convergence* is the definitive Souls experience: the world-design ambition of the first game, the systems-generosity of the second, and the combat clarity of the third, presented as one coherent authorial vision rather than a museum. It is designed for two audiences simultaneously — the **returning veteran** who wants their accumulated literacy to be rewarded with *new* depth, and the **newcomer** for whom this is entry zero and who must never be required to know a single piece of prior lore to understand this one.

**The newcomer rule:** no plot beat, item description, or NPC line may require knowledge of Dark Souls 1–3 to be comprehensible. Prior lore appears as *echo and rhyme*, never as *prerequisite*.

## 1.2 — World Premise: The Convergence

### 1.2.1 The Cosmological Failure

The First Flame did not fail once. It failed **repeatedly, and each failure was patched.**

Every time the Flame was linked, the Age of Fire was extended — but extension is not renewal. A linked Flame does not burn forward; it burns *backward*, consuming the memory of the world to produce more light. After enough linkings, the world ran out of **future** and began burning its own **past**.

Three ages, which were never meant to touch, were pulled into a single gravity well:

| Age | Source fiction | Its defining force | What it contributes to the Convergence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Age of Fire / The Fading** | Lordran (DS1) | **Souls** — memory, identity, the differential that makes a thing *itself* | The Flame, the Lords, the bonfire network, true poise of a world that refuses to end |
| **The Age of Want** | Drangleic (DS2) | **Want** — desire without object, hunger that outlives the hungry | The Curse, Vendrick's throne, Adaptability, Life Gems, Ascetics, hexes |
| **The Age of Ash** | Lothric (DS3) | **Ash** — residue, the unburnt remainder, the Unkindled | Coalescence, Weapon Arts, Embers, the refusal of the throne |

The Convergence is the moment these three ceased to be sequential and became **superimposed**. It is not a cataclysm that happened *to* the world; it is what the world looks like when its timeline has been used as fuel.

### 1.2.2 The Physical Symptom: Bleed-Through

Where ages overlap, geography contradicts itself. The world's name for this is **bleed-through**, and it is the engine of every biome in §5.

Observed bleed-through phenomena (all are *gameplay* systems, not set dressing):

1. **Spatial contradiction** — a flooded Drangleic cathedral stands inside a Lothric cemetery; its nave is 400 m long and 40 m wide, and both measurements are correct depending on which age is dominant at that hour.
2. **Chronal eddies** — zones where dead enemies respawn without a bonfire rest (the *Convergence Clock*, §7.5). Players learn to read these as danger indicators.
3. **Gravitic inversion** — the endgame region is literally the world turned inside-out: you walk on the underside of the sky.
4. **Ghost-strata** — three versions of the same NPC exist in the same room at different opacities. Only the dominant one can be spoken to; the others whisper spoilers of your own possible futures.
5. **Ashfall weather** — periodic grey snow that is not snow. During ashfall, fire damage is +10% and dark damage is −10% (regional modifier, visible in the HUD status ribbon, §9.2).

### 1.2.3 The Metaphysics of the Triad

The Convergence is sustained by three substances which are also three *verbs*:

| Essence | Verb | Nature | Mechanical expression |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Souls** | *To remember* | Souls are what a thing is *made of* and what it is *called*. Lose them and you forget your own shape. | Currency, level-ups, Soul Memory, the DS1 humanity system |
| **Want** | *To desire* | Want is the hole left when a soul is spent. It is not evil; it is *appetite with the object removed*. | Hex fuel / Resonance (§4.5), Want Shards, Life Gems (which heal by *wanting* the body whole), the Throne ending |
| **Ash** | *To remain* | Ash is what survives burning. It has no memory and no desire — only persistence. | Embers, flask allocation, Weapon Art ranks, the Unkindled body, the refusal ending |

**The central thesis of the game, stated as design:** *Souls make you real. Want makes you move. Ash makes you last. Every system in this game asks which of the three you are spending.*

### 1.2.4 The World's Name

The converged landmass has no single name, because names are a Souls-function and the Convergence is eroding them. Different populations call it:

- **Lothranor** — the scholarly/Lothric usage (Lothric + Lordran + Drangleic). Used in menus and the world map title.
- **The Grey Cradle** — used by the Eclipse Sanctuary priesthood.
- **Ash-and-Want** — used by the common dead. The title of the game comes from this.

## 1.3 — Player Identity: The Ashen Hollow

### 1.3.1 Origin

You wake in a **mass grave of the unburnt** — the Ashen Barrens (§5.4, Region 01) — where the ash of a thousand failed linkings has compacted into something with a spine. You are not resurrected. You are **accumulated**.

> **Opening crawl (the only narration in the game; ~85 words, spoken by a woman's voice over black):**
>
> *"Once, the Flame was linked, and the Age of Fire was prolonged. Again, the Flame was linked, and the world grew thin. A third time, and a fourth — until the ages could no longer remember their order, and fell into one another.*
> *Now there is no Fire, no Dark, no Want. There is only the grey between them, and the ash that will not stop falling.*
> *You are that ash. You are also the hole it fell into.*
> *Rise, Hollow. Choose what burns."*

The crawl is skippable and never repeated. It is the game's only hand-holding.

### 1.3.2 What The Player Is, Precisely

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| **Race/Type** | Unkindled-Hollow hybrid: ash-body stabilized by a Dark Sigil |
| **Canon name** | None. Referred to as *Ashen One*, *Hollow*, *the Grey*, *Unkindled*, depending on speaker and faction |
| **Sex/age** | Player-selected; body decays visibly with Hollowing (§1.5) |
| **Voice** | Silent protagonist. All player agency is expressed through gesture, choice, and violence. (Design rule: **the player never speaks a line of dialogue.** We keep the series' discipline here — a talking protagonist would collapse the "vessel" fantasy that the endings depend on.) |
| **Starting classes** | 10 (§2.2), each mapping to one of the three ages, which is the first *silent* narrative choice the player makes |
| **Signature mark** | The **Dark Sigil** — a black brand over the sternum that multiplies and spreads |

### 1.3.3 The Double Curse

The Ashen Hollow bears **both** curses of the prior ages, and this is the mechanical heart of the character:

1. **The Hollowing Curse (Souls/DS1-DS2).** You lose your shape as you die. Hollowing is cosmetic + a small number of *fiction-locked* interactions.
2. **The Dark Sigil (Want/DS3).** Each Sigil is a debt. Sigils are *acquired*, not given, and each one:
   - increases Hollowing gained per death,
   - increases maximum **FP** by 6 and **Want** capacity by 1 (a real, tangible *buff* — the game never lets purity be strictly better),
   - locks one NPC questline branch open and one closed,
   - is required for the *Usurper of the Abyss* ending (§1.7.3),
   - and, at 8 Sigils, makes you a permanent invasion target for one specific covenant hunter (§8.4).

> **Design intent:** the Sigil is the game's clearest statement that in this world, *damnation is a resource*. Players who min-max will deliberately take Sigils. The narrative does not punish them for it — it *agrees* with them, and that agreement is the horror.

### 1.3.4 Player Motivation Loop

```
WAKE (ash) → LEARN (region logic) → SPEND (souls/sigils/healing choice)
    ↑                                              ↓
REMEMBER (bloodstain, NPC drift, world decay) ← FAIL or SUCCEED
    ↓
CONVERGE (world state advances regardless of outcome) → CHOOSE (ending)
```

The critical property: **the world advances whether you win or lose.** The Convergence Clock (§7.5) ticks on death, on boss kill, and on Ascetic burn. There is no "pause the apocalypse by exploring." This converts the classic Souls backtracking loop into a *pressure* loop.

## 1.4 — Factions, Pantheon & Powers

### 1.4.1 The Powers That Remain

| Power | Origin age | Status in Convergence | Mechanical footprint |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The First Flame** | Lordran | Reduced to a **cinder-heart** the size of a fist, kept in the Eclipse Sanctuary. It is dying *again*, and dying faster because it is now burning three timelines. | Bonfire network; Estus; kindling; the Relink ending |
| **The Throne of Want** | Drangleic | Empty, and *hungry*. It has begun summoning claimants on its own. | Want resource; Hexes; Ascetics; the Throne ending |
| **The Abyss / The Deep** | Lordran + Lothric | The Abyss and the Deep were the same thing seen from two ages; they have merged into **The Want Beneath**, a physical inverted region. | Dark damage, Darkwraith & Abyss covenants; the Usurper ending |
| **The Old Dragons** | All three | Nearly extinct. One survives as a **Remnant** — a fragment of a dragon that is also a fragment of three dragons. | Dragon Remnants covenant; dragon-scale upgrades; boss #3 |
| **The Gods / The Church** | Lordran + Lothric | The gods are dead; the *churches* survived them and have merged into the **Sanctuary of the Eclipse**, which now worships the phenomenon rather than any deity. | Miracles; Blue Sentinels; Warrior of Sunlight covenants |
| **The Serpents** | Lordran | **Frampt** and **Kaathe** are the same serpent, seen from opposite ends of its own life. It does not know this yet. It will, during your playthrough. | The two covenant brokers; the meta-narrative spine |

### 1.4.2 The Serpent Problem (Narrative Spine)

The single most important NPC in the game is **Ouroboros, the Frampt-Kaathe**, a primordial serpent whose body runs the length of the world map — literally. Players cross its spine three times without realizing (§5.5, the Great Serpent Viaduct).

- **First meeting (early):** It presents as **Frampt** — genial, offers to consume surplus items for souls, urges Relinking the Flame.
- **Second meeting (mid):** It presents as **Kaathe** — cold, offers Dark Sigils, urges the Usurpation.
- **Third meeting (late):** Both mouths speak at once, and it realizes it is one animal. The player's response to this determines the hidden ending (§1.7.4).

This is the game's structural thesis made into a character: **the Convergence is not a disaster that happened to the world; it is the world discovering it was always one thing.**

### 1.4.3 The Kindled Echoes (Ending-Critical NPCs)

Five NPCs whose survival and quest completion determine ending *variants*. They are the game's only fail-soft narrative state.

| # | Name | Role | Region | If alive at endgame | If dead/failed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ysolde, the Last Keeper** | Fire Keeper of the cinder-heart | Eclipse Sanctuary | Enables *Kindled Relink* (sub-variant A) | Relink ending becomes *Cold Relink* (sub-variant B) |
| 2 | **Sir Aldwin of the Empty Shield** | Blue Sentinel knight, guardian of the hub | Eclipse Sanctuary → Frozen Crypts | Enables Blue Sentinel rank 3; gives **Oathstone** | Hub becomes invadable in NG+; loses unique armor |
| 3 | **Greymouthe, the Hexwife** | Vendor/tutor of dark magic | Sunken Cathedral → The Want Beneath | Unlocks 6 endgame Hexes + Resonance rank 3 | Hex school caps at Resonance 2; Throne ending loses its epilogue |
| 4 | **Vhenn, the Ash-Smith** | Blacksmith; handles infusions, transposition, Ascetic binding | Eclipse Sanctuary | Unlocks Ascetic rank 4 + **Ashen Brand** item | Ascetics cap at rank 3; hidden ending unreachable |
| 5 | **The Small Sun** | A child carrying a shard of sunlight; Warrior of Sunlight anchor | Ember Keep | Sunlight covenant rank 3; enables *co-op relink* (you may summon 2 phantoms into the final fight) | Final fight is solo-locked |

**Rule:** no Echo can be killed by accident. Each has an explicit, deliberate kill-condition (a specific dialogue choice or a specific invasion), and each death is *announced* by a world event (a bell, a darkening, an ashfall). The player always knows what they did.

## 1.5 — Hollowing: The Body As A Ledger

Hollowing is a 0–99 counter that is **primarily cosmetic and narrative**, with exactly three mechanical hooks. This restraint is deliberate: DS2's hollowing (max HP loss) was widely resented; DS3's (purely cosmetic + Yuria quest) was under-felt. We take the middle.

### 1.5.1 Acquisition

```
On death:  Hollowing += 2 × (1 + DarkSigilCount)
On drawing dark power from the Frampt-Kaathe:  Hollowing += 5, DarkSigilCount += 1
On purging (Purge Stone / Ysolde's rite):  Hollowing = 0  (Sigils are NOT removed)
```

### 1.5.2 The Three Mechanical Hooks

| Hook | Effect | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Appearance** | 0–19: pallor. 20–39: ashen skin, visible Sigil. 40–59: cracked porcelain flesh, one eye dark. 60–79: exposed bone at joints, hunched idle pose. 80–99: fully skeletal face, ember-glow in the ribcage, ash particles shed while moving. | Body-as-save-file. Other players see your history in co-op. |
| **Hollowed Weapon Synergy** | At ≥15 Hollowing, **Hollow-infused** weapons gain +1 scaling tier and their bleed/dark buildup +25%. At ≥60, they gain a passive Weapon Art charge. | Rewards the "embraced damnation" build path; makes hollowing a *choice* with upside. |
| **NPC gating** | Certain NPCs refuse you below/above thresholds; two questlines only open at ≥40 (Greymouthe) and one only at 0 (The Small Sun's final gift). | Fiction and system reinforce. |

**Explicitly NOT a mechanic:** max HP reduction, Estus reduction, stat loss, forced PvP. Hollowing never makes you weaker in a fight.

### 1.5.3 The Sigil Board (UI, §9.6)

The character screen shows a **torso diagram** with up to 8 Sigil slots. Each Sigil is a black knot. Hovering shows:
`SIGIL III — "Taken from Kaathe's mouth at the Sunken Cathedral." +6 FP · +1 Want · Hollowing ×4 per death · Required: Usurper of the Abyss`

Sigils have **named provenance**. Every one of them remembers where you got it. This is the game's most concentrated piece of environmental storytelling: your own body is a quest log.

## 1.6 — Narrative Structure & Beats

### 1.6.1 Three-Act Architecture

| Act | Regions | Beats | Player power | Narrative question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I — The Grey Waking** | Ashen Barrens, Eclipse Sanctuary, Grave of Names | Wake; learn death; meet Ysolde, Vhenn, Frampt; light the Sanctuary | 1 region, 1 boss, 0 Sigils | *"Am I supposed to save this?"* |
| **II — The Three Crowns** | Sunken Cathedral, Ember Keep, Winter's Veil, Dragon Remnant Shrine (+ 4 optional) | Retrieve the **three essences**: the *Soul of Memory* (Sunken), the *Heart of Want* (Ember), the *Ashen Cinder* (Winter's). Meet Kaathe. First Sigil offered. | 9 regions, 12 bosses, 0–4 Sigils | *"Who asked me to?"* |
| **III — The Want Beneath** | Grave of Kings, Iron Eclipse, The Want Beneath (inverted), Convergence | All three essences can be *held*, *spent*, or *refused*. Ouroboros revealed. Final boss. | 14 regions, 22 bosses, any Sigils | *"What do I want?"* |

### 1.6.2 The Essential Throughline: Three Keys, Three Doors

The endgame requires the player to have decided what to *do* with the triad. Physically, the Convergence has three doors in the final arena antechamber, and the player opens them in any order (or not at all):

| Door | Requires | Opens |
|---|---|---|
| **The Cinder Door** | *Ashen Cinder* (from Winter's Veil) | The Flame's last cinder-heart chamber → Relink path |
| **The Hollow Door** | *Heart of Want* (from Ember Keep) | The Throne of Want → Throne path |
| **The Dark Door** | *Soul of Memory* + ≥5 Dark Sigils | The Want Beneath → Usurper path |

All three are always physically present. **The game never tells you which is "good."** NPCs argue. The Serpent argues with itself. The item descriptions contradict one another on purpose.

### 1.6.3 Lore Delivery Rules

1. **No cutscene exceeds 90 seconds.** The longest is the final boss's phase-3 transition (88 s, skippable after first view).
2. **No lore is delivered during combat** except boss roars that are diegetic and telegraph moves.
3. **Item text is the primary lore channel.** Every unique item has 40–90 words. Boss weapons have 120–180.
4. **Every region contains at least one "readable room"** — a space where the story is told entirely by object placement, with zero text. (Reference: the nursery in the Frozen Crypts, the 40 chairs in the Sunken Cathedral.)
5. **Contradiction is a feature.** Two items may give incompatible accounts of the same event. We ship a **canon document** internally, but never reconcile in-game.
6. **Names rhyme, never repeat.** No character may share a name with a prior-trilogy character. Echoes are *echoes*: "Sir Aldwin" is not "Sir Artorias," and the game will not wink at you about it.

## 1.7 — Endings

Four distinct cinematic outcomes across three named endings, plus two sub-variants. All are reachable in a single playthrough **except** that the hidden ending requires conditions that lock out one other ending.

**Global rules for all endings:**
- The final boss must be defeated. Endings are chosen **after** the fight, in the Convergence chamber, with no timer.
- The player may **walk away from the chamber** and return later; the world state is frozen at the moment of the final kill.
- Each ending grants a unique achievement, a unique title on the character card, and a unique weapon available on NG+ (never a stat advantage).
- Endings are **not** ranked as better/worse anywhere in the game or its marketing.

---

### 1.7.1 ENDING I — *Relinking the Flame*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Requirements** | Possess the **Ashen Cinder**; open the **Cinder Door**; speak to Ysolde at the cinder-heart (or reach the Kiln-equivalent alone). No Sigil count requirement — this is the "default" ending and is always available. |
| **Core choice** | At the cinder-heart you may (a) link alone, or (b) if ≥3 Kindled Echoes survive, link *with witnesses* — they burn alongside you. |
| **Philosophical position** | Continuity. The world deserves its next age even if it must be paid for with you. This is the DS1/DS3 "link the fire" answer, and the game treats it as **noble and insufficient.** |

**Sub-Variant A — *The Kindled Flame*** (≥3 Echoes alive, choice (b))
The cinder-heart accepts five lives instead of one. The burn is *slower*. Cinematic: you kneel; Ysolde places her hands over yours; Aldwin sets his empty shield down as a stepping-stone; Vhenn feeds the fire his own hammer; Greymouthe whispers a hex that makes the flame eat darkness instead of light; The Small Sun walks in and simply sits down beside you. The fire takes them all, gently, over 74 seconds. Ash stops falling for the first time in the game. **The world's sky turns from grey to a bruised gold — and the credits roll over a sunrise that is visibly, mechanically *temporary*.** A final title card: *"And so the Flame was linked. And so it began to fade."*

- **Rewards:** Achievement *Kindled*; title **The Last Ember**; NG+ unlock **Ysolde's Veil** (cosmetic + a Miracle that cannot be obtained otherwise).

**Sub-Variant B — *The Cold Relink*** (Ysolde dead or choice (a))
You burn alone. The fire does not want you — you are ash, and ash is what fire leaves, not what it eats. Cinematic: 51 seconds of you failing to ignite, then *forcing* it, tearing the Sigils out of your own chest one at a time and using them as kindling. The last thing on screen is your ribcage, open, holding a flame the size of a coin. The sky does not change. Ash continues to fall. The title card: *"And so the Flame was linked. It did not notice."*

- **Rewards:** Achievement *Cold Kiln*; title **Unkindled Still**; NG+ unlock **Cinderbrand Sigil** (a Weapon Art that costs HP instead of FP).

**Design note.** Sub-variant B is the *worse* outcome fictionally and the *better* outcome mechanically for a dark build (Cinderbrand is a top-tier Weapon Art). We do this deliberately: the game must never let morality and optimization align, because the moment they do, the player stops making choices and starts making calculations.

### 1.7.2 ENDING II — *The Throne of Want*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Requirements** | Possess the **Heart of Want**; open the **Hollow Door**; sit upon the Throne in the Sanctum of Want. Optional modifier: speak to Greymouthe first (she offers to sit *with* you) and/or have Hollowing ≥60. |
| **Core choice** | Once seated, the Throne asks a single question, delivered as three interaction prompts with no text: **REIGN**, **REFUSE**, **DEVOUR**. (Only REIGN and DEVOUR produce this ending; REFUSE routes to Ending III or the hidden ending.) |
| **Philosophical position** | Stewardship through appetite. If the world is a hunger, then someone must become the thing it hungers *for*. This is the DS2 answer — Vendrick's throne, Nashandra's want, the Empty King — and the game treats it as **compassionate and monstrous.** |

**The Cinematic (96 s — the game's longest, and it is intentionally too long)**

You sit. The camera pulls back and *keeps* pulling back — through the throne room wall, through the region, up through the strata of the world map, until the entire 14-region map of §5 is visible as a single grey spiral. Then the spiral begins to *inhale*. Every bonfire in the world flares once and goes out, in sequence, nearest to farthest — 78 fires, over 40 seconds. The player watches the world's save-state die.

The camera returns to your face. Your eyes are gone. In their place, two black knots. Your mouth opens and the voice that comes out is every NPC you ever spoke to, layered. The Throne is not a seat; it is a **mouth**, and you have volunteered to be its teeth.

Final shot: the ash stops falling — because there is nothing left above to fall *from*. Fade to black. Title card: *"And so the Want was throned. It is still hungry. It will always be still hungry."*

**Mechanical consequence (diegetic, shown before you commit):** a confirmation prompt reads *"The world will not end. Nothing in it will ever change again. Sit?"* — this is the only confirmation prompt in the entire game.

**Variant — *The Devouring Throne*** (choose DEVOUR instead of REIGN)
You do not sit. You *eat* the Throne. Cinematic is shorter (58 s) and far more violent: you tear the Throne apart with bare hands and consume it, and the Want relocates into you. The world does not freeze — it begins to *hunt*. The final shot is you walking out of the Sanctum into a world where every living thing turns to face you. This variant is the bridge to the expansion (*The Want Beneath*), and it is the only ending that leaves the world in an active, worsening state.

- **Rewards (REIGN):** Achievement *Throned*; title **Vessel of Want**; NG+ unlock **Vendrick's Patience** ring (+4 Want regen, −20% Estus speed).
- **Rewards (DEVOUR):** Achievement *Devourer*; title **The Hunger Crowned**; NG+ unlock **Thronebreaker Hex** (a Hex that consumes the target's Want meter entirely).

### 1.7.3 ENDING III — *Usurper of the Abyss*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Requirements** | Possess the **Soul of Memory**; **≥5 Dark Sigils**; complete Greymouthe's questline to rank 3; open the **Dark Door**; descend into **The Want Beneath**. |
| **Core choice** | At the bottom of the inverted world you find the Flame — not a cinder, but a *wound*. You may **extinguish** it (classic dark ending) or **take it** (usurpation). Taking it is this ending. |
| **Philosophical position** | Sovereignty. If the gods spent the world to keep the fire lit, then the debt belongs to the dark. This is the DS3 Usurpation lineage crossed with the DS2 Dark Chasm, and the game treats it as **liberating and terminal.** |

**The Cinematic (82 s)**

You reach into the wound and pull the Flame out. It is small. It fits in one hand. The camera holds on it for eleven seconds — long enough to be uncomfortable.

Then you *close your fist*, and the Flame does not go out. It goes **in**. Every Dark Sigil on your body ignites at once, and the shot pulls back to reveal that your Sigils were never brands — they were **stitches**, holding a shape shut, and now the shape is opening.

The world inverts. The inverted sky of The Want Beneath becomes the ground; the ground becomes sky. Every region of the map, one by one, turns from grey to black — but *lit* black, like a negative photograph with the sun still in it. The final shot is you standing on the underside of the world, holding a flame that no longer needs fuel because it now burns **desire itself**.

Title card: *"And so the Flame was taken. There is no Age of Fire. There is no Age of Dark. There is only the Long Want, and you at its head."*

**Epilogue beat (only if Greymouthe survives):** she is standing beside you. She was never an NPC — she is revealed as the *previous* Usurper, who abdicated. She hands you her chime and walks into the dark, relieved. This is the game's only instance of an NPC leaving the world permanently *without dying*.

- **Rewards:** Achievement *Usurper*; title **Sovereign of the Want Beneath**; NG+ unlock **Abyssal Crown** (head slot; grants 3 Resonance stacks at start of every Hex chain — extremely strong, and it permanently hides your face, which many players will read as a punishment).

### 1.7.4 HIDDEN ENDING — *The Severance* (aka "The Unkindled Warden")

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Requirements** | ALL of: (1) Vhenn alive → obtain the **Ashen Brand**; (2) all five Kindled Echoes alive; (3) all three Severance parts taken from the final boss (§6.4 — the only boss with a 3-part severance); (4) covenant rank 3 in **at least two** covenants; (5) refuse all three doors (do NOT open Cinder, Hollow, or Dark) and instead use the Ashen Brand on the **Convergence seam** itself, located behind the throne room in a room with no door that only appears once conditions 1–4 are met. |
| **Discoverability** | Zero tutorialization. Foreshadowed by: Ouroboros's third conversation, three item descriptions, one gesture (***Sever*** — awarded at Dragon Remnants rank 2), and the fact that the final boss has a visibly glowing seam on its chest in phase 3 that no attack except the Brand interacts with. |

**The Cinematic (69 s)**

You do not link, throne, or usurp. You **cut**.

The Ashen Brand is a knife made from the spine of the Frampt-Kaathe — you took it from a serpent that was one animal pretending to be two, and you use it to sever the Convergence itself. The three ages come apart. Not into the past — they cannot go back — but into **three separate, simultaneous, smaller worlds**, each with its own sky.

The shot splits into a triptych: on the left, a Lordran-shaped grey land with a bonfire in it; in the center, a Drangleic-shaped green sea with a throne in it; on the right, a Lothric-shaped gold city with a cliff in it. Each has one figure walking in it. Each figure is you.

The world map of §5 folds along its seams and becomes three maps. The camera pulls back to the Eclipse Sanctuary, which is the only place that survives intact — because the Sanctuary is where the eclipse is, and an eclipse is *the only thing that exists in all three ages at once*.

You sit down at the bonfire. Ysolde sits beside you. You do not burn. The five Echoes do not burn. The ash keeps falling, but now it falls on three worlds instead of one, and each of them is thin enough to survive.

Title card: *"And so the Flame was neither linked, nor throned, nor taken. It was divided. And you were left to watch all three of them fade — slowly, and in order."*

- **Rewards:** Achievement *Severance*; title **The Warden of Three Greys**; NG+ unlock **Ouroboros Spine** (a whip-sword whose Weapon Art is literally a seam-cut that ignores armor); the world map in NG+ is rendered as a triptych; a fourth bonfire appears in the Sanctuary that warps you to any of the three sub-worlds (a fast-travel system that no other ending grants).

**Design note.** This ending is the reward for *playing the game's systems rather than its story*: it requires the severance mechanic (Vol. 3/6), the covenant mechanic (Vol. 8), the crafting NPC chain (Vol. 7), and the refusal of all narrative offers. It is the GDD's statement that in a Souls game, **mechanics are the deepest lore.**

## 1.9 — Tone, Language & Audio-Narrative Direction

| Domain | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Register** | Late-medieval English inflected with ecclesiastical Latin and one invented liturgical tongue (*Ashen Cant*) used only by the Sanctuary priesthood. No modern idioms. No contractions in item text. |
| **Sentence length** | Item text: average 14 words per sentence, max 3 sentences. NPC dialogue: max 6 lines per interaction, no branching mid-line. |
| **Forbidden words** | "magic system", "level up", "quest", "boss", "NPC", "damage" — never in-fiction. Diegetic equivalents: *art*, *ascend*, *errand*, *warden*, *the dead*, *wound*. |
| **Voice direction** | Every NPC is recorded **tired**. No NPC in this game is excited. The single most excited line in the script belongs to The Small Sun, who is a child, and it is the line that breaks players. |
| **Music** | Full orchestra + choir, but the choir is **mixed lower than is comfortable** — you should always feel you are missing words. Boss themes introduce a fourth interval stack on the Convergence's leitmotif. Silence is a weapon: three regions have no music at all until a boss trigger. |
| **Sound design** | Ash has a sound. It is the sound of very distant paper. It is present in every outdoor region and absent in every interior, which trains the player to know when they are safe without ever being told. |
